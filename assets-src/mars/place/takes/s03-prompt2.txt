Image 1 is a square, straight down (nadir) orbital photograph of canyon land on Mars, north at the top, about 18 km across. Its forms are right: every cliff, ledge, spur, mesa, gully and the shadowed hollow at its upper left are where they must be. Its light is wrong: it is lit too brightly and too evenly, with an orange midday look.

Image 2 is an oblique aerial photograph taken low over the very same land, looking west. It gives the light, the colours and the air of this land at this hour: a low Sun about 20 degrees above the horizon; dark brown and umber layered bedrock; long soft shadows; only the faces that look toward the Sun glow warm orange; the canyon lies deep in shadow with thin warm dust haze in it; the high plateau is rust orange under haze.

Task: repaint image 1 in the light, the colours and the air of image 2, as one seamless nadir orbital photograph with the same framing, the same scale and north at the top.

1. Every form stays exactly where it is and keeps its size and outline: every cliff, ledge, spur, mesa, gully and ridge. Nothing is moved, added or taken away.

2. The Sun stands low in the upper left (north west). Faces that look toward the upper left are lit warm orange. Faces that look toward the lower right, and the ground behind cliffs, ridges and mesas, lie in long soft shadows that fall toward the lower right.

3. The rock is dark brown and umber as in the near half of image 2, with rust orange only on sunlit faces and on sunlit flat plateau tops. No bright orange plain, no flat even light.

4. Hollows and the feet of cliffs are in shadow, very dark with the ground in them still to be made out.

5. Sharp natural detail everywhere, the same sharpness over the whole picture. No smear, no blur, no streaks, no etched lines, no bright outlines, no blue, no white, no pure black. No cloud, no water. No new craters.

A photograph from orbit: no horizon, no sky, no text, no border, no vignette.
