Image 1 is a square, straight down (nadir) orbital photograph of rocky canyon land on Mars in low evening light, north at the top, about 18 km across. It is an enlargement and therefore soft.

Image 2 is an oblique aerial photograph taken low over the same land, looking west. It shows what this land is made of: dark brown layered bedrock, cliffs with strata, spurs, buttresses, gullies, talus aprons, rubble.

Task: redraw image 1 as a sharp nadir orbital photograph with the same framing, the same scale and north at the top.

1. Everything keeps its place and its brightness: every cliff, ledge, face, shadow, gully and sand drift of image 1 stays where it is, as light or as dark as it is. Nothing is added that changes the layout, nothing is moved.

2. Add the fine detail a camera resolves at this scale: fractures and joints in the bedrock, ledges and strata on the cliff faces, boulders and rubble at their feet, small gullies, ripples on the sand drifts.

3. No smear, no blur, no streaks, no flat cells, no etched lines, no bright outlines, no blue, no white. No pure black. No mist, no cloud, no water. No round pits, no fields of craters.

Light: a low Sun, soft long shadows, the same everywhere. A photograph from orbit: no horizon, no sky, no text, no border, no vignette.
