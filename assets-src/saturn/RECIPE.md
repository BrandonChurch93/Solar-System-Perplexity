# Saturn asset recipes

Every file under `public/assets/saturn/` and how it is made. All scripts run from the repo root. Work files stay under `out/work/saturn/`.

## `public/assets/saturn/<set>/wrap-color.jpg` (full 8192 x 4096, web 4096 x 2048)

- Tool: `python3 assets-src/saturn/wrap/light.py` then `python3 assets-src/saturn/wrap/build.py 12` (numpy, OpenCV). The 2x enlargement of the hero globe is `python3 tools/ml/upscale.py --in out/work/saturn/hero-globe.png --out out/work/saturn/hero-globe-2x.png --width 2304` (crop of the hero x 928..2080, y 200..1352).
- Inputs: `references/approved/saturn-hero.png`, `assets-src/saturn/fit.json`, `src/stops/saturn/curve.json`, `out/work/saturn/light-table.npy`.
- Prompt: none. No image model paints the globe.
- What it does: every hero pixel on the globe is put on the fitted spheroid and divided by the measured light (`light_at` in `wrap/geo.py`: curve by Sun cosine times the table by Sun cosine and latitude), which leaves albedo. Hero pixels are kept where the view cosine and the Sun cosine are high and no ring covers or shadows the globe. Elsewhere the map is the zonal albedo profile of the hero times a luminance detail tile cut from the hero's own bands, made periodic with 12 periods around the globe, so the far side has the same band structure and the same fine detail and no seam. The file stores sRGB of filmic(albedo); the shader decodes it with `inverseFilmic`.
- Take kept: the build after the light table (04:09 UTC). Earlier builds without the table left the terminator baked in.
- Checks: `python3 assets-src/saturn/check/ratio.py FILM OUT.jpg` on the judged hold: film over hero within about 0.2 stops for Sun cosine above 0 at every latitude. No pinched pole: the detail tile fades to the zonal profile above latitude 80.

## `public/assets/saturn/<set>/light.bin` (48 x 41 RGBA float32, same file in both sets)

- Tool: `python3 assets-src/saturn/wrap/light.py`.
- Inputs: `references/approved/saturn-hero.png`, `assets-src/saturn/fit.json`.
- Prompt: none.
- What it does: iterates the zonal albedo and the light curve on hero pixels outside the rings and the ring shadow, then stores hero / (zonal albedo x curve) by Sun cosine (48 columns) and latitude (41 rows, row 0 is the south pole). Rows the hero does not show are mirrored from the north. Also writes `src/stops/saturn/curve.json` (33 knots) and `light.json`.
- Checks: residual with the table, median 0.99 to 1.01 in every range of Sun cosine, 10th and 90th percentile 0.84 to 1.16.

## `public/assets/saturn/<set>/rings.bin` (full 32768 samples, web 16384, RGBA float32 with mip rows; two profiles)

- Tool: `python3 assets-src/saturn/rings/polar_view.py`, `rings/close_polar.py`, `rings/extract.py`, then `python3 assets-src/saturn/rings/build.py` (optional argument: the share of the synthetic gaps and lines in the close profile, 0.35). Azimuth gain: `python3 assets-src/saturn/rings/azimuth.py` writes `src/stops/saturn/azimuth.json`.
- Inputs: `references/approved/saturn-hero.png`, `references/approved/saturn-close.jpg`, `assets-src/saturn/fit.json`, `src/stops/saturn/close.json`.
- Prompt: none.
- What it does: both approved images are resampled to ring polar coordinates (radius by azimuth) with the fitted cameras. Columns are aligned and the median over azimuth gives the radial colour profile of each image. Above the resolution of the images the profile is continued with band limited fine structure whose strength per octave is taken from the profile's own finest octave, so the rings resolve into thousands of bands in the dive. Colour is stored in display space (filmic), alpha is coverage. Row 0 holds the profile, the following rows its mips (box filtered), so the shader can pick the level from the radial footprint and thin bands never alias.
- Close profile since 05:30 UTC: it is no longer taken by radius. `close/layout.py` and `close/profile.py` (see `layout.bin` below) give every pixel of the approved close image a band coordinate, and the profile is the median of the approved image along three families of straight lines across the bands (9, 7 and 5 parallel lines, aligned by shift), in scene values. `rings/build.py` resamples it through the warp `ANCH_C` to `ANCH_H` (close band coordinate to hero radius), so the same texture coordinate holds the same ring in both profiles. Run order: `rings/build.py` (writes the warp anchors), `close/layout.py 3 0.3 0.001 0.5 0.15`, `close/profile.py CHECK.jpg`, `rings/build.py`.
- Fine detail of the close profile since 06:55 UTC: the average over the parallel lines is kept below 8 px and multiplied by the detail of the median of three centre cuts (offsets -2, 0, 2 px) above it, with a quarter of its colour, so the film has the line sharpness of the approved image without its pixel grain and without coloured fringes.
- Checks: G13 on every dive and rise frame; G17 on the judged frames; `check/fine.py 0 FILM...` (rms above 1.5 px and 6 px in four boxes, approved first); ring display means against both approved images with `check/contrast.py`; `check/pair.py close FILM OUT.jpg` puts the approved close image over the film frame.

## `public/assets/saturn/<set>/sky-hold.webp` (full 3520 x 1980, web 1760 x 990, lossless)

- Tool: `python3 assets-src/saturn/sky/patch.py hold references/approved/saturn-hero.png out/work/saturn/sky-fill-hero-take1.png out/work/saturn/halo-only.png` (numpy, OpenCV), fill image from the workspace image tool.
- Model for the fill: `seedream_5`, 16:9, 2816 x 1584, reference image `references/approved/saturn-hero.png`.
- Prompt for the fill: "Edit image 1. Remove the ringed planet, its whole ring system and the small orange moon completely, and remove every trace of their glow and shadow. Fill the area they covered with deep space that continues the surrounding sky exactly: the same field of fine, pinpoint, slightly colored stars, the same faint dusty Milky Way band crossing the frame, the same near-black blue background, the same star sizes, density, brightness and grain. Keep everything else unchanged: same framing, same stars in the same places, same exposure and color. No planet, no rings, no moon, no sun, no glow, no nebula colors that are not already present, no text, no border. Wide 16:9 frame."
- What it does: where the hero shows sky, the patch is the hero minus the film's own glow (a render of the judged hold with the patch gain at 0, `halo-only.png`). Where the body, the rings or Titan cover the sky (mask from the fit: spheroid, the whole ring disc out to 2.45, moon circle, grown a little), only two things are taken from the generated fill: its stars (what stands above a local background by more than 2.5 to 5 times the noise, half desaturated, tinted and scaled to the hero's own stars) and half of its mid-scale brightness ripple. The background colour and level there are the hero's own sky carried inward (normalized blur at 70, 200 and 600 px), so no tint and no trace of the planet from the fill can show. A margin of 12.5 percent on every side lets the camera move in the hold. The margin is not a mirror: `python3 assets-src/saturn/sky/patch.py margin sky-hold.webp` (and the same with `sky-terrain.webp`) carries the star-free background outward and blurs it, moves the sparse bright stars of the frame outward by half a frame, and leaves crowded star clouds out, so no feature of the approved sky appears twice. Inside the approved border nothing changes (hold: largest difference 2 levels on 2.2e-7 of the pixels, terrain 1 level). `python3 tools/assets/mirrorcheck.py` is clean on both.
- Take kept: take 1 of 1. The fill's stars match the hero's in size at native size.
- Checks: hero over film sky medians 1.7 to 1.95 at gain 1, so `sky.json` gain 1.85. Share of star pixels: hero sky 0.0047, filled part 0.0040; star tint (R, G, B over luminance) hero 1.06, 0.97, 1.18, filled part matched to it. No baked star in any other asset.
- Round 3 clean-up, take 4 of 4 kept: `python3 assets-src/saturn/sky/clean.py hold out/work/saturn/keep/sky-hold-full-r2.webp` (the round 2 file as source, no generation; takes 1 to 3 under `out/work/saturn/keep/`). It splits the source into a star-free low layer and stars and then: rebuilds the margin from a reflected low layer blurred with distance, with the sparse stars moved by half a frame (no edge columns carried outward, so the pillars and streaks of round 2 are gone); heals the outline of the body mask, the dark blob at Titan's place (the dark ring around the moon the critics saw) and the approved lens streak's pixels in the sky from their surroundings (the film draws the streak itself, `streak.ts`); and scales the filled stars inside the tight body mask (spheroid, or ring plane radius under 2.345, blur 3) by 1 - (0.5 + 0.38 x (1 - real)), where `real` marks the hero's own stars, so the star count seen through the shadowed rings at the lower right is the approved one. Take 1 scaled only the inner gap (0.38), take 2 used the wide mask (a star-free band above the outer ring edge), take 3 the tight mask at 0.5.

## `public/assets/saturn/<set>/layout.bin` (640 x 480, 4 channels float32, same file in both sets)

- Tool: `python3 assets-src/saturn/close/layout.py 3 0.3 0.001 0.5 0.15 0.1 model` then `python3 assets-src/saturn/close/profile.py CHECK.jpg` (numpy, scipy sparse, OpenCV). Optional arguments of `profile.py` after the check name: REACH S0 SK SF (0.8 0.004 0.5 2.5), DETAIL (8 px), mask growth (9 px), shade sigma (12 px), fill weight (150), chroma of the fine detail (0.25), softening of the band coordinate under the dome (`SOFT`, -0.3). Argument 12 is `EVEN` (see `layout-even.bin`), argument 13 `CUTS` (5): the detail finer than `DETAIL` is the mean of 2 x CUTS + 1 cuts 2 px apart with the lowest and highest dropped, so the sand grain of the approved image is not drawn out into hairlines around the ring (with the median of three cuts two thirds of the grain became lines). `--trace` prints the solved band coordinate along the junction normals and the spread of channel 3 by ring radius.
- Inputs: `references/approved/saturn-close.jpg`, `src/stops/saturn/close.json` (fitted close camera), `src/stops/saturn/closeModel.json` (nested circle model from `close/model.py`), `src/stops/saturn/rings.json` (warp anchors), traced curves `out/work/saturn/close-outer.npy` and `close-limb.npy`, and the junction of globe and rings traced by hand in `profile.py`.
- Prompt: none.
- What it does: the approved close image is not a rigid ring about the globe, so the band layout of the close view is taken from the picture itself. The bands of the approved image are a family of nested circles whose centres move with the radius (`closeModel.json`); the shader moves the physical rings into that family exactly with `uClose` (`familyAt`), so the rings stay one plane with elliptic edges all the way down. `layout.py` solves the band coordinate on a grid seen from a layout camera 0.35 behind the fitted close camera with a 104 degree field of view; `profile.py` stores per cell: channel 0 the difference between that coordinate and the circle model (added late in the dive, `settle`), channel 1 a soft shade (blurred approved luminance over blurred luminance rebuilt from the profile, sigma 12 px, which carries the shadow of the globe on the rings and the dark zone under the globe), channel 2 a weight that is 1 inside the approved frame and fades to 0 outside it, channel 3 the signed distance in approved pixels to the junction of globe and rings (positive on the ring side, the traced junction continued in a straight line outside the frame, kept between -400 and 4000). The junction is 21 hand traced points from (1383, 718) to (2816, 989) joined by a monotone cubic (`PchipInterpolator`, 4 px steps) with three limb points in front. Between the junction and the inner edge of the B ring the approved image shows the fine stripes of the C ring that the solve does not reach, so there the band coordinate is laid in proportion: from -0.09 at the junction to 0.62 where the solved coordinate first crosses 0.62 along the junction normal (105 to 191 px), blended in over the first 40 px along the junction and the last quarter of the width. Where the approved image is darker than 0.04 in scene luminance the shade is taken against the local peak of the rebuilt profile (15 px dilation) instead of its blur, so the shadowed rings stay near black, and close to the junction the shade is continued along the junction normal. Ring fragments that the globe would hide (the inner disc and the far rings behind the dome) are drawn over the globe where channel 3 is positive, so the base of the globe in the film is the junction of the approved image. During the dive and the rise that overlay enters and leaves as one edge that runs parallel to the junction (`veil` and `sweep` in `look.ts`: the edge sits at channel 3 = `sweep` x (1 - veil)), so the globe lifts out of the rings instead of fading. Row 0 is the top of the image. The shader projects each ring fragment with the layout camera (`uLayoutM`) and reads the cell with a manual bilinear `texelFetch`.
- Checks: `out/work/saturn/CHECK.jpg` (approved over the rebuild from profile and shade), `close/layout_check.py`, `check/pair.py close FILM OUT.jpg`, columns of display values across the junction (approved against film) at x 1100, 1300, 1500, 1700.
