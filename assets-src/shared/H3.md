# H 3: counts, warm start, the clock of the player

Working notes of engine_profiler on branch `agent/engine-perf`. The rules are in the README section "The live player".

## What was built

- `src/core/counts.ts`: `Counter` wraps the calls of the GL context that make work and counts them: programs are `linkProgram`; textures are every upload (`texImage2D`, `texSubImage2D`, `texStorage2D`, the compressed and 3D forms, `copyTexImage2D`); buffers are `createBuffer`, `createFramebuffer`, `createRenderbuffer`, `createVertexArray`. `showcase.counts()` gives `{ programs, textures, buffers }` since the last call.
- `src/core/warm.ts` and `src/core/warm.json`: the warm start. In a hold the next stop's set is loaded (the loader frees the stop before first, so two sets stay the most) and each listed frame of the stop is drawn once with the real `drawFrame`, one per display frame, behind the picture: the real frame is drawn after it in the same task. Engine hooks: `engine.ahead(p, tier, last, all)` and `engine.warmDraw(frame, tier)`.
- `src/space/belt/belt.ts`: `keep(from)`, so the belt loaded in the hold before its travel is not freed by the frames before the travel.
- `src/director/modes.ts`: autopilot and the live page ask for the film's time as a real number at the display's rate; the warm start runs before the first frame and in each hold.
- `tools/warmcheck.ts` (`npm run warm`): the test, the maker of the list (`--write`, `--reset`), the proof that a warm draw changes no pixel (`--stills`, and `--self` in gate G3).

## Why a list of frames

A program is compiled when the first mesh that uses it is drawn, with the defines of that frame. A list of real film frames makes the same programs the film makes, with no second copy of any stop's code, and nothing under `src/stops/` is touched. A place picture is kept under a key that does not hold the frame, so the picture a warm draw makes is the one the film uses.

## Sets

`sets.maxLoaded` stays 2. In a hold only the stop's own set is needed, so the next one takes the place of the one before. Three sets are not needed.

## Capture and export

Capture and export never warm draw: `showcase.renderFrame` and the export page draw the asked frame alone, after its sets are awaited, so what is drawn there is fixed by the frame index. The warm start is called only by autopilot and the live page (`src/director/modes.ts`) and by `showcase.ahead`, which only `tools/warmcheck.ts` calls and which awaits every load before it draws. `tests/warm.test.ts` (in `npm test`, gate G2) fails when another caller appears. `npm run warm -- --self` (gate G3) and `--stills` hold that a still drawn after warm draws has the bytes of the same still drawn cold.

In the live player a warm draw happens in a hold only. When the next set arrives after the hold, the travel does its own work, as before the warm start (ruling of MAIL 422).

## The place of the samples inside a pixel (d198)

`src/core/jitter.ts` gives the offsets of a count less their own mean; `src/core/sampler.ts` `samplesFor` reads them. Old mean in pixels (x, y): 4 samples -0.1128 and -0.0754, 16 samples -0.0835 and -0.0314, 64 samples -0.0134 and -0.0114. Now nil to 1e-16 at every count from 1 to 256, and no offset further than 0.526 from the pixel's middle (count 52). Test: `tests/jitter.test.ts`.

Since 09892f3e (MAIL 731) each axis holds one sample in each of n equal strips of the pixel: the sample of rank k on an axis, ranked by the value of the old sequence, sits at (2k + 1 - n) / (2n). Before it the 16 samples lay in 4 clumps on x (gaps between neighbours 0.0195 to 0.2061 px, even would be 0.0625) and about 7 on y, so an edge near the vertical showed 4 grey steps of 15. Now every gap on an axis is 1/n, the mean is nil, no offset is further than 0.5 - 1/(2n) on an axis (the 0.526 above is gone), and the largest move of a sample is 0.090 px at 4 samples, 0.072 at 16, 0.025 at 64. The order of the samples is kept. A frame of one sample keeps its bytes; every frame of more than one sample changes. The sharpness reading G17 of the holds of Mars, Saturn and Uranus moved by 0.002 at most.

Who reads jitterX and jitterY: `src/core/engine.ts` 252 and 268 (`drawFrame` hands them to `renderSample`, which sets them as the camera's view offset, 296) and `src/stops/mercury/set.ts` 339 (asks `samplesFor(view.samples)[view.sample]` again and hands the pair to the globe's smear, `src/stops/mercury/globe.ts` 733 and 343, which takes the sample back to the pixel's middle). Three more read the same pair from the camera's view offset: `src/stops/venus/set.ts` 314 (`src/stops/venus/globe.ts` 319, back to the pixel's middle), `src/space/belt/belt.ts` 275 and `src/stops/earth/relief.ts` 310. All of them read the pair as the offset of this sample from the pixel's middle, which it still is. Not touched: `src/space/belt/belt.ts` 82 and 378 keeps a sequence of its own of the same form for the steps of a close rock's layer.


## The order of the samples in time (MAIL 783, 790): measured, left as it is

`src/core/sampler.ts` `samplesFor` gives sample i both the time slot i and offset i of the sequence. In that order the offset steps by the same part of a pixel from each sample to the next (x by -0.245 px, back every 4.08 samples), so for a single fine pattern (0.5 to 1.5 waves a pixel) there are speeds at which the samples all meet it at one place: by calculation the mean lets such a pattern through whole (share 1.00 at 24, 39 and 64 samples), where an order that repeats no step (Welch's: the powers of the smallest primitive root of the next prime) lets 0.46, 0.38 and 0.33 through at worst. That table measures the wrong thing for a picture. Earth 1870, review, master, 39 samples, drawn in both orders against two references of 156 samples (one in each order, a dev switch on the count, not committed): mean square error against the mean of both references 1.01 levels for the sequence's own order and 1.64 for Welch's (left third 1.12 and 1.54, right third 0.90 and 1.53); the two references differ by 0.88, and from the four differences the error of each alone comes to about 0.9 and 1.5 at 39 samples, 0.5 and 0.7 at 156. The sequence's own order is a lattice in time and place: it fails at single patterns finer than a pixel, which a picture hardly holds, and is exact for the broad ones, which it mostly holds. An order without steps that repeat spreads the same error over every pattern. So the pairing stays. The coloured dots of Earth's dive (1870, left) are not from it: with the shutter shut the cloud shows bright coloured specks of 1 to 3 px (bright runs along x: median 3 px), with it open each is drawn 8 px along x (median 10 px), in both orders alike.

## The far Sun's two loops (MAIL 695)
