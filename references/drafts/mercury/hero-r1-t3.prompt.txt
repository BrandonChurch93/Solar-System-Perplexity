Mercury as a whole world, large and dominant, composed like a dramatic film poster during arrival.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

THE GLOBE: unmistakably Mercury, the solar system's innermost planet, at close range. One whole round opaque globe entirely within the frame and near frame height. Scorched gritty charcoal, cool ashen grey, hints of hammered bronze, not fifty shades of yellow; a chiseled landscape of ancient low-reflectance plains, irregular nested old craters, Mercurian lobate contraction scarps and broad ochre lowlands. One broad degraded multi-ring Caloris-style basin and small bright fresh Kuiper-like impact craters whose irregular fine sprays of white ejecta streak over the dark rock. Long relief shadows reveal serrated rims. A huge white-gold photosphere, with a distinct curved edge and a thin rim of solar granulation just outside a compact hot core, is glaring nearby yet reads behind the planet and separated by open space. The Sun is the sole light and is well behind Mercury, so the world is dramatically backlit: a broad bright crescent on the side TOWARD the Sun, shadow covers at least sixty percent of Mercury, and no sun-facing crater slopes can have shadows thrown toward the Sun. Keep the sunlit crust blazing but textured with smoky neutral shadow filling the portion of the dark limb that remains visible. No uniformly glowing rim, no atmospheric rim; hard rocky lit edge.

FRAMING: 32 degree field of view at Mercury's equator from a lower forward camera. Full Mercury just left of center and up, with a stark illuminated arc stretching along the bottom and the right side. One huge solar arc rises from below the frame slightly to the right of Mercury, visibly separate, cutting a shallow wide white-gold arc across the bottom-right edge; the planet is lit by this sole light from the lower right behind it. Shadow at the top is large, deep and cool and upper rim darkly visible against the stars away from the solar glare. Richer shadowed greys, very little orange; pale Kuiper-like streaks cut diagonally across the cratered dayside.

SKY AND OPTICS: one physically opaque globe and no other bodies except Mercury and the single Sun if specified. Place the fine, dim Milky Way on the side of the frame away from the key light; the stars are small and pin sharp, with no stars through rock or in any surface shadow. Warm highlights, cool neutral charcoal shadows, very fine film grain, restrained anamorphic lens bloom and a low-key large-format film exposure. Infinite depth of field, fine geological detail even toward the horizon, restrained grade, never sharpened to a synthetic look. No atmosphere on Mercury, no fog, lava or floating debris. No rings or moons.

REFERENCES: image 1 is of Saturn, a different planet, used exclusively for contrast, color grade, lens, density of stars and rendering quality. Never copy Saturn's rings, globe, colors, Titan, moons, silhouettes, arrangement or composition. Image 2 is a real MESSENGER whole globe of Mercury for the shape and distribution of basins and fresh ray systems, not its exaggerated blue false color; image 3 is actual Kuiper ejecta, for the fine streaks of pale crushed rock over dark terrain. The real photos are subject references only, never style or composition references.
