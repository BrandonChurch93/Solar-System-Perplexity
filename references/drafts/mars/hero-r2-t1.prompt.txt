Entire Mars on approach, a dramatic film-poster frame with a dominant complete globe and only one small companion.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

LAYOUT: Image 1 controls only the geometric composition, phase, occlusion and the one Sun direction; re-render at film finish. Keep the whole, round, opaque Mars and its terminator and the compact scale, exact position and phase of the separate Phobos. Replace layout placeholder terrain and sky, and make the tiny moon irregular. Image 2 is Saturn, a different planet, purely for grade, contrast, lens character, star density and rendering quality. Never copy Saturn's globe, rings, colors, moons, Titan, or composition. Image 3 is the Mars Viking reference for geographical features only. Image 4 is HiRISE Phobos, used ONLY for irregular shape, crater and surface grooves. Ignore its original lighting, color, exposure, scale and orientation. Image 5 is the Mars Orbiter Camera dust reference; borrow only the natural thin shape of windblown Martian dust. No mission/documentary look.

MARS: This is the iconic whole red world at enormous scale, not a map or documentary. Rake light across the enormous relief of a divided Valles Marineris, the recessed parallel trenches sharply defined with fractured and terraced flanks. A cluster of giant but wide, short shield volcanoes and summit calderas climbs through shade to its west, including Olympus Mons and the Tharsis rises. The polar cap is brilliant scalloped white frost embedded in the north terrain, not pure white paint. Dry solid rock, deep red iron oxide and burnt ochre, occasional dark crimson volcanic fields. Exposed half full of sharp tactile geological details varying widely across scales, with no smearing in deep dark low-contrast shadow. In a sunlit basin away from the canyon, one strong locality of windblown DUST skims the ground in a flat fan turning around a large basin, curling fine parallel orange-ochre streaks like wind stirring powder directly on a table. All within a few kilometers of the surface and only an albedo tonal change against the ground, never tall smoke, cumulus, ground fire, a cloud object, or a discrete element. A narrow, very subtle amber scatter traces the sunward limb of this slightly hazy, completely opaque globe; its entire limb remains clean and smoothly spherical in the shadows.

CAMERA AND LIGHT: With the planet centered right, Phobos drifts at eight o'clock lower-left in entirely open sky. It must be small and dark; the off-frame Sun coming from upper left illuminates its left edge only. The right part of Phobos goes into shadow as on Mars. Show a deep night side on Mars' screen-right, with the huge canyon crossing from textured light into shade. Graze a low dust swirl high enough in the key-side basin that it stays within daylight.

FINISH: Shoot in a low-key large-format photochemical look with restrained warm glints and cool shadows. Allow darkness to overtake a large part of the sphere but retain readable crater detail for its low-key relief and keep the dark limb crisp. Pin-sharp subtle dark stars and a subdued diagonal arm of dusk-toned Milky Way, far dimmer than the illuminated surface. All stars are behind the bodies, never painted inside any silhouette, and the moon and globe are sharply focused, with no halo or drop shadow on either side of the moon; it is unlit where appropriate, not a gray pasted shape. It is an independently sized but clearly tiny charcoal gray irregular rocky moon, like a pitted broken potato with a small impact depression, far closer to the lens than Mars, about 2% of the frame width, never large and round. It is outside Mars' visible limb with a clean narrow gap. Fine real film grain, optical falloff, no flare lines or blown highlights.
