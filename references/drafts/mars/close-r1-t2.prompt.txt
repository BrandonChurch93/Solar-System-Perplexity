First-person flight low over the Valles Marineris wall at dawn, neither a satellite view nor a shot from the canyon bottom. The view clears the rift rim and sees the opposite escarpment across a staggeringly broad dry basin, then the curved Martian planetary horizon beyond it, running across the frame in the upper third. Russet soil lies over broken, sharply resolved thin ledges of ochre sandstone-like sediment and dark volcanic strata. Wind-carved vertical gullies interrupt the horizontal bands; old landslides spread into massive aprons at their base. Delicate volumetric dawn mist and dust collect in the canyon's low points as streaks with independent shadows and gaps over the dry floor; no water or oceans, and the mist never hides the cliff geology. The low horizon glows pale butterscotch into a narrow cooler band and near-black starry upper sky, with scale conveyed by deep canyon layers and strong foreground parallax. No terrestrial foliage or weather cloud ceiling.

CAMERA AND LIGHT: Bank across the head of a major side chasma, just above its near right fault escarpment, with the wide main rift opening across most of the left field. The far escarpment and the planet's subtle curved horizon both recede across the scene at different depths. The Sun just below the far upper-left horizon, with a subtle dawn rim; grazing light cutting across the opposite cliff and catching narrow shelves while leaving the near right wall's scalloped geology dark and sharply readable; luminous gold where the lowest mist lies in the sunlight, cooler gray-violet in dark hollows. Mild optical halation, but no white wash, dreamy-looking softening, or towering decorative fog.
