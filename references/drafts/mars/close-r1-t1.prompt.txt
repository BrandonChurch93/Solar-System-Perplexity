Mars, low flight over the Valles Marineris walls at first dawn.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. First-person flight low over the Valles Marineris wall at dawn, neither a satellite view nor a shot from the canyon bottom. The view clears the rift rim and sees the opposite escarpment across a staggeringly broad dry basin, then the curved Martian planetary horizon beyond it, running across the frame in the upper third. Russet soil lies over broken, sharply resolved thin ledges of ochre sandstone-like sediment and dark volcanic strata. Wind-carved vertical gullies interrupt the horizontal bands; old landslides spread into massive aprons at their base. Delicate volumetric dawn mist and dust collect in the canyon's low points as streaks with independent shadows and gaps over the dry floor; no water or oceans, and the mist never hides the cliff geology. The low horizon glows pale butterscotch into a narrow cooler band and near-black starry upper sky, with scale conveyed by deep canyon layers and strong foreground parallax. No terrestrial foliage or weather cloud ceiling.

CAMERA AND LIGHT: Sweep parallel to the southern rim at just above rim level; near left cliff occupies the lower left and falls sharply into a wide trough that runs diagonally to the far center-right, where opposing layered cliffs emerge through multiple veils of dust. Put the nascent sun just out of shot on screen right, its fine amber side light carving the cliff strata and projecting separated long cliff shadows across the trapped floor mist. Use a slight bank without tilting the horizon excessively. Let a few tiny distant stars show above the steep dawn brightness gradient.

EXPOSURE AND AIR: Use one Sun, out of frame, without an unmotivated lens streak. Color separates physically illuminated butterscotch dust from strongly shadowed cool violet basalt. Deep space dark and restrained above the narrow horizon gradation, with pin-sharp faint stars in only the darkest part of the upper sky. Not a dusty brown wash: expose rocky shelves crisply through clear intervening air. Everything in sharp focus from near fractured wall to the planetary horizon. Fine large-format film grain and natural vignette.
