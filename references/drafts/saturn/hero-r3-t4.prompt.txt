Saturn, the whole planet inside its full ring system, large and dominant in the frame, composed like a film poster.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

LAYOUT (image 1): image 1 is the layout frame for this exact shot: a geometrically exact but crude render with a blank black sky. Re-render this same shot at the finish of a top-budget film. Keep from image 1, precisely: the framing; the size, position, shape and tilt of the globe and of the ring ellipse; which arc of the rings passes in front of the globe and which passes behind it; the day side, the terminator and the night side of the globe; the shape and position of the globe's single black shadow across the rings; the fine ring-shadow lines across the globe; and the position, size and lighting phase of Titan. The globe is completely opaque. Do not add a visible Sun, a second light source, extra moons or extra shadows.

THE GLOBE: unmistakably Saturn, not Jupiter: a vast, serene, hazy golden world. Dozens of parallel bands in butterscotch, honey, straw, champagne and cream under a pearly upper haze, shading to cool blue-grey at the north pole. Within that calm order, detail no telescope has ever resolved: long silk-fine filaments and ripples along every band edge, rows of tiny scalloped waves, small pale oval storms with soft shadowed rims, towering haze layers whose shadows give the cloud deck real depth, all crisp at the terminator where low sunlight rakes across the cloud tops. No heavy brown belts and no red spot. A thin luminous atmosphere line glows gold-white along the sunlit limb and fades to a faint blue thread around the night side, which is not black but faintly lit by warm light reflected from the rings.

THE RINGS: thousands of razor-fine concentric bands of water ice in champagne, pale gold, rose-grey, pearl and silver, no two alike, crisp to the far edge: the dusky translucent inner C ring, the dense brilliant B ring, the broad dark Cassini Division as a clear near-black gap, the A ring with the hair-thin Encke gap, and the thread of the F ring outside. Faint stars show through the thinner rings. Nearest the camera the bands resolve into their true texture, a fine grain of countless ice particles, each catching a pinpoint glint of sunlight like frost at sunrise, with sunlight scattering through the ice so the bright rings glow softly from within. Everything in sharp focus from foreground to far distance: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh, no floating debris cloud.

SKY: Titan as in image 1: a small hazy orange sphere with a soft fuzzy atmosphere edge, its lit side facing the same Sun. Behind everything, deep black space with a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint and the dusty band of the Milky Way crossing the frame, rich but kept darker than the sunlit planet so the exposure feels real.

LIGHT: one hard, distant Sun, off-frame, exactly as in image 1. Warm gold on the day side, a long soft terminator, deep shadow with detail still readable, strong contrast.
