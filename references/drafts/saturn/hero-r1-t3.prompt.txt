Saturn, the whole planet and its full ring system, large and dominant in the frame, composed like a film poster.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

COMPOSITION: seen from about 30 degrees above the ring plane on the planet's shadow side quarter, the globe centred, tilted so the rings cross the frame on a strong diagonal from lower left to upper right and nearly touch both edges of the frame. Symmetrical, monumental, poster-like.

THE GLOBE: a golden banded sphere in butterscotch, honey, cream and amber with cooler blue-grey toward the north pole. Every band edge is alive with fine turbulent eddies, scalloped waves and small oval storms, with layered haze giving the cloud deck real depth.

THE RINGS: resolved into thousands of razor-fine concentric bands of ice in champagne, pale gold, rose-grey and silver, with the broad dark Cassini Division as a clear near-black gap between the bright B ring and the A ring, and the thin Encke gap near the outer edge. Sunlight passes through the ice from behind: the rings glow translucent, the thin ones luminous, the dense ones darker, and the nearest part of the rings resolves into glittering ice grains throwing tiny sparkles.

LIGHT: strong three-quarter backlight. The Sun is out of frame to the upper right and behind the planet, so about 60 percent of the globe is lit in warm gold with a brilliant thin rim of light on the limb and a glowing atmosphere edge, and the rest falls into deep shadow softened by ring-shine. The rings cast a tight set of fine dark parallel shadow lines across the lit hemisphere. The globe casts a hard-edged black shadow across the rings behind it.

DISTANCE: Titan hangs small in the lower right distance, a hazy orange sphere with a soft fuzzy atmosphere edge. Behind everything the Milky Way band crosses the frame among dense stars.

Use them for features only. Do not copy their flat documentary look, their empty black backgrounds or their compositions.
