Flying just above Saturn's rings: an endless glittering field of ice sweeping away to the horizon in fine concentric grooves, with Saturn enormous behind it. The viewer feels the scale.

Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. THE RINGS UP CLOSE: not a ground and not a floor. Saturn's rings are a thin, flat swarm of separate bodies of water ice orbiting in vacuum, only a few tens of metres thick. In the nearest foreground the swarm is resolved: hundreds of free-floating, slowly tumbling ice boulders and icebergs, house-sized down to fist-sized, white and pale blue-grey water ice with frosted, fractured faces, a few dusted pale tan. They hang in empty black space with clear dark gaps between them and nothing underneath; some drift a little above or below the sheet. Each is lit hard on its sunward side and black on the other, the nearer ones throwing shadows only onto the bodies behind them. A few body-lengths farther on, the gaps close up visually and the swarm becomes one continuous, softly glowing granular sheet, then thousands of razor-fine grooves and bands in champagne, pale gold, pearl and silver curving away to a hard, distant horizon exactly as laid out in image 1. Sunlight passes through the ice so the sheet glows from within. There is no solid surface, no gravel bed, no shadows lying on a floor, no wet or glossy look, no mirror reflection of the planet in the rings, and no coloured stones: only ice. Everything in sharp focus from the nearest iceberg to the horizon: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh.

SATURN: unmistakably Saturn, colossal, rising behind the ring plane and filling its side of the frame: a serene, hazy golden world of gas in dozens of parallel bands of butterscotch, honey, straw, champagne and cream under a pearly haze, with silk-fine filaments, ripples and soft eddies along every band edge. All soft vapour: no pockmarks, no crater-like dimples, no hard-rimmed spots, no heavy brown belts. A thin luminous atmosphere line along the sunlit limb. Thin dark shadow lines cast by the rings cross the globe near its equator.

SKY: deep black space. Because the Sun is in frame the stars are few and faint, showing only far from the Sun; no bright Milky Way. No stars show through the globe or through the rings.

LIGHT: the single Sun in frame, small and distant but blinding. The ring plane is lit at a low angle from ahead and to the left: ice rimmed with light and glowing translucent from within. Saturn's sunward limb glows gold; its night side is lit only by warm ringshine.

CAMERA AND GRADE: large-format film frame, ultra-wide anamorphic lens held low over the ring plane, deep focus. The Sun blooms softly and throws one clean horizontal anamorphic streak; no other flares or glints. Cool blue-black shadows against warm gold highlights, very fine film grain, gentle natural vignette. Low-key and high contrast.
