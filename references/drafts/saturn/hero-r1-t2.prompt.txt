Saturn, the whole planet and its full ring system, large and dominant in the frame, composed like a film poster.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

COMPOSITION: a low, dramatic three-quarter view from only about 12 degrees above the ring plane and close in. The immense rings fill the lower half of the frame as a vast tilted plain rushing toward the camera from the globe, their near edge passing under the lens; the globe rises behind them, centred slightly left, filling most of the frame height, like a golden world rising over a sea of ice.

THE GLOBE: a golden banded sphere in butterscotch, honey, cream and amber with cooler blue-grey toward the north pole and a hint of the polar hexagon. Every band edge is alive with fine turbulent eddies, scalloped waves and small oval storms, with layered haze giving the cloud deck real depth. A thin luminous atmosphere line glows along the sunlit limb.

THE RINGS: resolved into thousands of razor-fine concentric bands of ice in champagne, pale gold, rose-grey and silver, with the broad dark Cassini Division as a clear near-black gap between the bright B ring and the A ring, and the thin Encke gap near the outer edge. Near the camera the bands resolve into countless glittering grains and boulders of water ice throwing tiny sparkles of sunlight; farther away light scatters through the ice so the thinner rings glow translucent.

LIGHT: the Sun itself is in frame at the far right edge, a small, intensely bright white disc with a natural lens flare and bloom, just above the ring plane. It rakes across the rings so they sparkle, lights the right two thirds of the globe, and leaves a deep shadowed crescent on the left with a faint glow of ring-shine. The rings cast a tight set of fine dark parallel shadow lines across the globe. The globe casts a long hard-edged black shadow across the rings on the left side.

DISTANCE: Titan hangs small in the upper left, a hazy orange sphere with a soft fuzzy atmosphere edge. Behind everything the Milky Way band arcs across the frame among dense stars.
