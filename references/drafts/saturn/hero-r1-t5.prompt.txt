Saturn, the whole planet and its full ring system, large and dominant in the frame, composed like a film poster.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

COMPOSITION: the camera floats about 25 degrees above the ring plane, slightly off-axis, so the rings are tilted wide open and sweep in a great ellipse from the lower left foreground around the globe to the upper right. The globe sits just right of centre and fills roughly half the frame height; the near arc of the rings passes close beneath the camera and runs out of frame at the lower left, huge in the foreground.

THE GLOBE: a golden banded sphere in butterscotch, honey, cream and amber with cooler blue-grey toward the pole. Every band edge is alive with fine turbulent eddies, scalloped waves and small oval storms, with soft upper haze giving the cloud deck visible depth. A thin luminous atmosphere line glows along the sunlit limb.

THE RINGS: resolved into thousands of razor-fine concentric bands of ice in champagne, pale gold, rose-grey and silver, no two alike, with the broad dark Cassini Division as a clear near-black gap between the bright B ring and the A ring, and the thin Encke gap near the outer edge. In the foreground the bands break up into glittering grains of ice that sparkle with tiny points of sunlight; light scatters through the thinner rings so they glow translucent.

LIGHT: the Sun is out of frame to the upper left. The globe is lit from the side with a soft terminator on its right third and a faint ring-lit glow on the night side. The rings cast a tight set of fine dark parallel shadow lines across the globe's southern hemisphere. The globe casts a hard-edged black shadow across the rings behind it.

DISTANCE: Titan hangs small in the upper right, a hazy orange sphere with a soft fuzzy atmosphere edge, half lit. Behind everything the Milky Way band crosses the frame on a diagonal among dense stars.
