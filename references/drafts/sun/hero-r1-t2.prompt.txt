THE SUN: unmistakably our Sun, a living star, every square kilometre of it in violent motion. Its surface is a raging ocean of fire: deeply textured granulation resolved across the entire disc, millions of boiling convection cells in molten gold, amber, tangerine and deep orange, separated by darker sinking lanes, with fibrous chromospheric spicules giving the surface a furred, seething texture near the edge. Expose for the surface so every cell stays readable: it is never blown out to flat white or yellow. Strong limb darkening carries the disc from a hot golden centre to a deep blood-orange and crimson edge, so the globe reads as a huge three-dimensional sphere. Dark sunspot clusters, each a black umbra ringed by a fibrous bronze penumbra, sit sunk into the surface. White-hot active regions blaze around them as tangled knots of brilliant magnetic filaments and bright faculae.

AT THE EDGE: off the left limb a vast eruptive prominence is tearing free, a twisted rope of glowing orange and magenta plasma billowing outward into a coronal mass ejection, its strands catching light from the surface below. Along the rest of the limb, rows of smaller prominence loops and flame-like spicules. The corona streams out in long, fine pearly-gold rays and helmet streamers, fanning across the dark left half of the frame and fading far out into space.
