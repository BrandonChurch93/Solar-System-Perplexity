The Sun, the whole star large and dominant in the frame, composed like a film poster: the complete disc left of centre, filling about three quarters of the frame height, seen from slightly below its equator, with a wide sweep of dark space on the right for the corona and the eruption.

THE SUN: unmistakably our Sun, a living star, every square kilometre of it in violent motion. Its surface is a raging ocean of fire: deeply textured granulation resolved across the entire disc, millions of boiling convection cells in molten gold, amber, tangerine and deep orange, separated by darker sinking lanes, with fibrous chromospheric spicules giving the surface a furred, seething texture near the edge. Expose for the surface so every cell stays readable: it is never blown out to flat white or yellow. Strong limb darkening carries the disc from a hot golden centre to a deep blood-orange and crimson edge, so the globe reads as a huge three-dimensional sphere. Dark sunspot clusters, each a black umbra ringed by a fibrous bronze penumbra, sit sunk into the surface. White-hot active regions blaze around them as tangled knots of brilliant magnetic filaments and bright faculae. A broad, irregular sunspot group sits in the lower-left quadrant of the disc, each spot a different size and shape, ragged and asymmetric, never a row of identical round holes.

AT THE EDGE: off the right limb, low on the disc, a vast eruptive prominence is lifting free: an irregular, ragged, open curtain of glowing orange and rose-magenta plasma threads, torn and uneven like smoke in a gale, partly draining back toward the surface, never a neat closed loop or handle shape. Along the rest of the limb, low rows of small prominences and flame-like spicules. The corona is one continuous, soft, pearly-gold halo that thins smoothly into long faint streamers and helmet streamers, densest near the star and fading gradually into space, with no separate fans or bundles of straight rays.

SKY AND LIGHT: the Sun is the only light source and is itself the subject. Deep black space with a dense field of fine pin-sharp stars and a faint dusty band of the Milky Way crossing the right of the frame behind the corona, all far dimmer than the corona. Everything in sharp focus. Natural bloom around the hottest regions only; no lens streaks. Low-key grade outside the disc, deep blacks, fine film grain, gentle vignette.
