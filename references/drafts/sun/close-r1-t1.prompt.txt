Skimming low over the surface of the Sun, a few hundred kilometres above the boiling photosphere, the camera racing forward. THE SURFACE: a molten sea of convection cells, each cell a domed, churning upwelling of incandescent gold and orange plasma the size of a country, edged by darker, sinking red-brown lanes, the pattern shrinking in perspective into hazy distance toward the horizon. Every cell holds finer structure: rolling filaments, bright points, rippling heat. Expose for the surface so the detail is never blown out to white.

THE SUNSPOT: in the mid-ground left, a vast sunspot opens like a dark canyon in the surface, a deep near-black umbral floor hundreds of kilometres down, ringed by long glowing bronze and orange penumbral filaments that stream inward over its rim like cliffs of fibrous fire. Bright granulation crowds right up to its edge.

OVERHEAD: towering plasma loops arch out of the surface and over the camera, glowing threads of orange and white-gold plasma anchored in bright active regions near the sunspot, coronal rain drizzling down their legs in fine bright beads, their light spilling onto the surface below. Thin glowing haze and spicules form a furred layer above the surface. Above the horizon, black space with a faint field of pin-sharp stars, far dimmer than the surface.
