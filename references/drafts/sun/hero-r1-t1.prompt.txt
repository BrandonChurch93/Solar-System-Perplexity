THE SUN: unmistakably our Sun, a living star, every square kilometre of it in violent motion. Its surface is a raging ocean of fire: deeply textured granulation resolved across the entire disc, millions of boiling convection cells in molten gold, amber, tangerine and deep orange, separated by darker sinking lanes, with fibrous chromospheric spicules giving the surface a furred, seething texture near the edge. Expose for the surface so every cell stays readable: it is never blown out to flat white or yellow. Strong limb darkening carries the disc from a hot golden centre to a deep blood-orange and crimson edge, so the globe reads as a huge three-dimensional sphere. Dark sunspot clusters, each a black umbra ringed by a fibrous bronze penumbra, sit sunk into the surface. White-hot active regions blaze around them as tangled knots of brilliant magnetic filaments and bright faculae.

AT THE EDGE: enormous prominences and plasma arcs leap off the limb. On the upper right, one colossal arch of braided, twisting plasma, dozens of Earths high, glowing orange and rose-magenta, its strands lit from within and fraying into fine threads, with plasma raining back down along its legs. Smaller hedgerow prominences and spikes stand along the rest of the limb like flames. Around it all, a streaming pearly-gold corona of fine radial streamers and long helmet streamers reaches far out into space, fading gradually into the dark.

SKY AND LIGHT: the Sun is the only light source and is itself the subject; nothing else is lit. Deep black space beyond the corona with a dense field of fine pin-sharp stars and the faint band of the Milky Way, all much dimmer than the corona, present but never competing. Everything in sharp focus. Natural bloom around the brightest active regions and the limb, one soft anamorphic horizontal streak running through the brightest active region. Low-key grade outside the disc, deep blacks, fine film grain, gentle vignette.
