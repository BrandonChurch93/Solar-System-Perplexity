THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

FRAMING AND GEOMETRY: whole-body hero portrait seen from an equatorial approach in space through a distortion-free cinema lens so the globe is perfectly round, crisp-edged, and dominant in the 16:9 widescreen frame, positioned slightly right of center (occupying roughly three-quarters of frame height). Venus is a solitary terrestrial planet completely wrapped in thick atmosphere: NO rings, NO ring arcs, NO moons anywhere in the frame. The globe is 100% opaque with a clean, continuous limb all the way round; zero stars ever show through the dark side of the planet. Do not include a visible Sun disc in frame.

THE GLOBE: unmistakably Venus: a luminous golden world completely veiled in vast, planetary-scale V-shaped chevron cloud bands of sulfuric acid sweeping from the equator toward smooth, bright champagne-cream polar collars. Within those sweeping golden, amber, honey, straw, and warm ivory cloud streams, resolve extraordinary three-dimensional vapour structure beyond any spacecraft photograph: stacked altocumulus-like cloud decks at multiple altitudes, silky wind-sheared filaments, fine wave trains, soft curling vortices, and deep sculpted cloud canyons casting long shadows across lower haze shelves near the terminator. Every feature reads strictly as soft, billowing atmospheric vapour and haze—no rocky surface, no crater-like pits, no dimples, no hard-rimmed spots, and no Jupiter-like narrow stripes or red spot. Along the sunlit right edge, the thick upper atmosphere glows as a brilliant, luminous golden-white rim arc tapering softly toward the poles.

NIGHT SIDE AND LIGHTNING: the left half of the globe lies beyond a wide, soft, gradual terminator in deep low-key shadow. The night side is not pure flat black: thick dark slate-bronze cloud silhouettes remain faintly readable in twilight scatter, and in two or three small regions deep on the night side, faint intra-cloud lightning flickers softly from beneath heavy cloud decks—tiny, diffused warm amber-white storm pulses muffled inside thick clouds, never surface city lights, never lava cracks, and never sharp cartoon bolts drawn on the surface.

SKY, LIGHT AND CAMERA GRADE: one hard distant Sun off-frame to the right illuminates the right half of Venus and casts every cloud shadow leftward into the terminator. Open space to the left and around the globe holds a dense field of pin-sharp stars and the delicate, dusty band of the Milky Way angling across the dark sky, properly exposed for the bright sunlit planet so the stars and Milky Way remain subtle and far dimmer than the sunlit clouds. Photographed on large-format anamorphic film: the bright golden right limb sends a gentle warm optical bloom and a faint horizontal anamorphic streak toward the right edge; cool blue-black shadows balance warm golden highlights, with very fine film grain and subtle natural vignette.
