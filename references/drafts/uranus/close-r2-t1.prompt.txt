A low skimming flight over the upper haze layers of Uranus, the camera just above the cloud tops, the planet's vast curved horizon crossing the frame, the viewer feeling the immense scale.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

THE SHOT: the camera flies a few hundred kilometres above the cloud tops, heading toward the equator, the horizon nearly level with a gentle bow across the lower third of the frame. The horizon of Uranus is a huge, gently curved arc crossing the lower part of the frame. Below, the atmosphere falls away in a deep gradient from bright, luminous cyan haze near the horizon down to dark ocean blue and indigo in the depths directly beneath the camera, seen through many stacked translucent haze layers with real volumetric depth, each layer a slightly deeper blue. This is an ice giant, not Earth: there are no cumulus clouds, no puffy white cloud banks, no ocean and no land. Instead, delicate long cloud streaks of pale cyan-white methane ice haze, extremely thin and gauzy, combed by high winds into long parallel filaments that run for thousands of kilometres along the bands toward the horizon, soft and wispy like high cirrus, some casting faint soft shadows on the haze deck below. It is all soft vapour: no pockmarks, no crater-like dimples, no hard-rimmed spots, no solid ground. Along the horizon the thin limb of atmosphere glows cyan against black space.

THE RINGS: high overhead, filling the upper half of the frame, the thin narrow rings of Uranus span the sky as one great arch: about a dozen hair-fine, separate concentric ring arcs that rise from the horizon at one side of the frame, curve high across the sky and descend toward the horizon at the other side, the camera seeing them from slightly below their plane. They are dark charcoal threads where in shadow and gleam silver-white where they catch the sunlight, with black sky between them; the outermost epsilon ring is slightly wider and brightest. They are thin and dark, nothing like Saturn's broad bright rings. The rings are opaque: no stars through them.

LIGHT: the small, distant Sun is off-frame to the left, low, raking across the cloud tops from the left so every filament has a bright left edge and a long shadow to the right; a soft pale flare veil comes in from the left edge only. One Sun only. Long shadows from the cloud filaments, sharp cold rim light on the haze tops.

SKY: black space around the ring arch with a dense field of very fine, pin-sharp stars and the faint band of the Milky Way. Expose for the sunlit clouds: the stars are delicate and much dimmer than the atmosphere.

CAMERA AND GRADE: photographed like a large-format film frame through an ultra-wide anamorphic lens, tilted up so the sky and rings take more of the frame. Everything in sharp focus from foreground to horizon: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh, no floating debris, no streaks in the sky other than the rings. Deep blue-black shadows against luminous cyan and white highlights, very fine film grain, gentle natural vignette. Low-key and high contrast, with large areas of deep shadow and a few brilliant highlights.
