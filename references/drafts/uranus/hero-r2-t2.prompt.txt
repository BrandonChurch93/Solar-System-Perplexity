Uranus, the whole ice-giant planet inside its thin ring system, large and dominant in the frame, composed like a film poster.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

LAYOUT (image 1): image 1 is the layout frame for this exact shot: a geometrically exact but crude render with a blank black sky. Re-render this same shot at the finish of a top-budget film. Keep from image 1, precisely: the framing; the size, position and shape of the globe; the narrow, nearly vertical ellipse of the rings (Uranus is tipped on its side); which arc of the rings passes in front of the globe and which passes behind it; the day side, the terminator and the night side of the globe; and the position, size and lighting phase of the moons. The globe is completely opaque with a crisp, clean edge all the way round. Do not add a visible Sun disc, a second light source, extra moons or extra shadows.

THE GLOBE: unmistakably Uranus: a glowing pale cyan and turquoise world of deep cold gas, luminous as if lit from within its haze. Soft, broad latitudinal banding in aquamarine, turquoise, pale teal and ice-blue, very subtle, under a thick pearly upper haze, and a bright, luminous white-cyan polar cap of haze glowing over the sunlit pole. Within that calm, almost featureless serenity, detail no telescope has ever resolved: long silk-fine streaks of bright methane cloud stretched along the bands, a few small brilliant white cloud plumes casting tiny shadows, delicate ripples and wave trains at the band edges, and stacked translucent haze layers that give the cloud deck real depth near the terminator. It is all soft vapour: no pockmarks, no crater-like dimples, no hard-rimmed spots, no dark storms, no heavy stripes. A thin luminous cyan atmosphere line glows along the sunlit limb. The night side is not pure black: it is a deep ocean blue, faintly readable.

THE RINGS: thin, dark, narrow rings, nothing like Saturn's broad bright rings: about a dozen razor-thin separate ringlets of dark charcoal ice and rock with wide black gaps between them, the outermost epsilon ring slightly wider. They are NOT bright outlines: most of their length is dim and dark grey, barely brighter than space, and they only flare to a soft silver gleam along the short stretch where they catch the sunlight most directly, so their brightness varies continuously along each arc and fades into shadow where the planet's shadow falls on them. Behind the globe they are hidden; in front of the globe they cross it as fine dark lines, and they cast hair-thin shadow lines on the clouds. The rings are opaque: no stars through them. Everything in sharp focus: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh, no floating debris.

MOONS: the two small moons as in image 1: small grey icy worlds (Miranda with its patchwork of cliffs and grooves, Ariel bright and cratered), crisp and sharp, their lit sides facing the same Sun as the planet.

SKY: deep black space with a dense field of very fine, pin-sharp stars and the dusky band of the Milky Way crossing the frame. Expose for the sunlit planet: the stars and Milky Way are delicate and much dimmer than the globe, present in open space but never competing with the planet. The Sun is far away here, so the light is cold, clean and crisp.

LIGHT: one hard, distant Sun, off-frame, exactly as in image 1. Bright luminous cyan on the day side, a long soft terminator where the haze layers are most sculpted, deep blue shadow with detail still readable.

CAMERA AND GRADE: photographed like a large-format film frame through an anamorphic lens. The off-frame Sun sends only a soft pale veil of flare in from the sunward corner; no long horizontal streak across the frame. Deep blue-black shadows against luminous cyan and white highlights, very fine film grain, gentle natural vignette. Low-key and high contrast, with large areas of deep shadow and a few brilliant highlights.
