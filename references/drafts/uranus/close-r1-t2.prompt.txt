A low skimming flight over the upper haze layers of Uranus, the camera just above the cloud tops, the planet's vast curved horizon crossing the frame, the viewer feeling the immense scale.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

THE SHOT: the camera flies a few hundred kilometres above the cloud tops. The horizon of Uranus is a huge, gently curved arc crossing the frame nearly level, slightly bowed, in the upper third of the frame, the planet filling most of the frame below. Below and ahead, the atmosphere falls away in a deep gradient from bright, luminous cyan haze near the horizon down to dark ocean blue and indigo in the depths directly beneath the camera, seen through many stacked translucent haze layers with real volumetric depth, each layer a slightly deeper blue. Over this, delicate long cloud streaks of bright white-cyan methane ice, combed into parallel lines by high winds and running toward the horizon, each casting a faint long shadow on the haze below in the low sunlight. It is all soft vapour: no pockmarks, no crater-like dimples, no hard-rimmed spots, no solid ground. Along the horizon the thin limb of atmosphere glows cyan against black space.

THE RINGS: overhead, the thin narrow rings of Uranus rise steeply from the horizon into the sky as a set of about a dozen hair-fine, separate ring arcs, dark charcoal threads where in shadow and gleaming silver-white where they catch the sunlight, with black sky between them. They are thin and dark, nothing like Saturn's broad bright rings. The rings are opaque: no stars through them.

LIGHT: the small, distant Sun sits just on the horizon at the far left of the frame as a brilliant white point with bloom and a horizontal anamorphic streak; the clouds facing the camera are back-lit and rim-lit, with long shadows reaching toward the camera. One Sun only. Long shadows across the cloud streaks, sharp cold rim light on the haze tops.

SKY: black space above the horizon with a dense field of very fine, pin-sharp stars and the faint band of the Milky Way. Expose for the sunlit clouds: the stars are delicate and much dimmer than the atmosphere.

CAMERA AND GRADE: photographed like a large-format film frame through an ultra-wide anamorphic lens. Everything in sharp focus from foreground to horizon: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh, no floating debris. Deep blue-black shadows against luminous cyan and white highlights, very fine film grain, gentle natural vignette. Low-key and high contrast, with large areas of deep shadow and a few brilliant highlights.
