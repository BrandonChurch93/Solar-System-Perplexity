Uranus, the whole ice-giant globe huge and dominant in the frame, filling about seventy percent of the frame height, composed like a film poster, its thin rings so large around it that they run out of the top and bottom of the frame.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

THE GLOBE: unmistakably Uranus: a glowing pale cyan and turquoise world of deep cold gas, luminous as if lit from within its haze. Soft, broad latitudinal banding in aquamarine, turquoise, pale teal and ice-blue, very subtle, under a thick pearly upper haze. Over the sunlit pole, placed exactly where image 1 shows the pale bright patch near the sunlit limb, a bright polar cap formed as a soft luminous hood of pale white-cyan haze that brightens gradually toward the pole, with no hard edge, bordered at its lower margin by a faint, slightly brighter collar band; the hood blends smoothly into the banding below. Within that calm serenity, detail no telescope has ever resolved: long silk-fine streaks of bright methane cloud stretched along the bands, a few small brilliant white cloud plumes casting tiny shadows, delicate ripples and wave trains at the band edges, and stacked translucent haze layers that give the cloud deck real depth near the terminator. It is all soft vapour: no pockmarks, no crater-like dimples, no hard-rimmed spots, no dark storms, no heavy stripes, no spotlight effect. A thin luminous cyan atmosphere line glows along the sunlit limb. The night side is not pure black: it is a deep ocean blue, faintly readable.

THE RINGS: not white wires and nothing like a diagram: thin, dark charcoal bands of ice and dust, nothing like Saturn's broad bright rings. About a dozen separate narrow ringlets of slightly different widths, the outermost epsilon ring the broadest and most substantial, the inner ones hair-fine, with black gaps between them. Against the sky they are mostly dim, a soft dark grey barely brighter than space, their brightness varying along each arc. Where they cross in front of the bright globe they show as thin DARK lines silhouetted against the glowing clouds, and they cast hair-thin shadow lines on the cloud tops. They catch the sunlight only in a few short, brilliant glints of silver along the sunward arc; everywhere else they stay dark. Behind the globe they are hidden. The rings are opaque: no stars through them. Everything in sharp focus: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh, no floating debris.

MOONS: as in image 1: two small grey icy moons, Ariel bright and cratered, the nearer and larger one, Umbriel darker and smaller and farther away, crisp and sharp, each shown at exactly its phase from image 1, lit by the same Sun as the planet.

SKY: deep black space with a dense field of very fine, pin-sharp stars and the dusky band of the Milky Way crossing the frame. Expose for the sunlit planet: the stars and Milky Way are delicate and much dimmer than the globe, present in open space but never competing with the planet. The Sun is far away here, so the light is cold, clean and crisp.

LIGHT: one hard, distant Sun, off-frame, exactly as in image 1. Bright luminous cyan on the day side, a long soft terminator where the haze layers are most sculpted, deep blue shadow with detail still readable.

CAMERA AND GRADE: photographed like a large-format film frame through an anamorphic lens. The off-frame Sun sends only a soft pale veil of flare in from the sunward corner; no long horizontal streak across the frame. Deep blue-black shadows against luminous cyan and white highlights, very fine film grain, gentle natural vignette. Low-key and high contrast, with large areas of deep shadow and a few brilliant highlights.
