A low pass over planet Earth: the camera flies high in the stratosphere, about thirty kilometres up, just above the tallest storm tops, the curved horizon of the planet crossing the frame, the viewer feeling the immense scale of the world below.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

THE SHOT: looking sideways along the coast with the camera banked; the horizon is a gentle curve running diagonally across the upper part of the frame from left to upper right, black space above it; the Sun is off-frame to the left.

SCALE (critical): everything is at true physical scale for a camera thirty kilometres up. The horizon is hundreds of kilometres away and only gently curved. The thunderheads rise about fifteen kilometres from the sea, so their anvil tops sit just below the camera: the nearest ones loom large in the foreground and mid-ground, and with distance they shrink quickly into a fine carpet of tiny clouds long before the horizon. Nothing rises anywhere near the horizon line or into space.

THE SURFACE: deep sapphire ocean with a real, recognizable coastline far below: the southern coast of Italy, Sicily and the Strait of Messina, with Mount Etna and the Apennine mountains, the deep blue Mediterranean, and the turquoise shallows of the Gulf of Gabes in the distance. Turquoise and jade shallows over sand banks and reefs, white surf lines, river mouths spilling silt plumes into the sea, green-gold land with real terrain relief. Islands, reefs and land lie flat at sea level and cast no shadows; only clouds cast shadows. A blinding sun glint blazes on the water, a molten white-gold path across the sea in which every wave and current line shows as fine silver texture. Towering cumulonimbus thunderheads with flat anvil tops and overshooting domes stand like mountain ranges, sculpted in volumetric detail, their sunward sides blazing white-gold and their far sides in cool blue shadow, each casting a long shadow across the lower clouds and the sea, the shadow pointing directly away from the Sun. Fields of small cumulus and thin cirrus streaks add layered depth. All cloud is soft vapour: no pits, no dimples, no hard-rimmed spots.

THE HORIZON: the curved limb of the Earth, with the atmosphere glowing electric blue in thin layered shells, from a bright white-cyan line at the horizon through deep cobalt into black space. Above it, black space with fine, faint, pin-sharp stars, far dimmer than the sunlit planet; no bright Milky Way and no nebula anywhere near the Sun's direction.

LIGHT: one hard, distant Sun off-frame to the left and low, cross-lighting the scene from the side, so each thunderhead is lit on its left and throws a long shadow to the right; the sun glint sits on the sea in the left part of the frame. Low sun, so every cloud edge is rim-lit and every shadow is long. Everything in sharp focus from foreground to the horizon: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh, no floating debris.

CAMERA AND GRADE: photographed like a large-format film frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, through an anamorphic lens, wide. A soft warm veil of flare and one horizontal anamorphic streak tied to the brightest point of the sun glint on the water. Cool blue-black shadows against warm gold highlights, very fine film grain, gentle natural vignette. Low-key and high contrast. No spacecraft, no station hardware, no window frame, no solar panels, nothing in the foreground but the planet.
