A low pass over planet Earth: the camera flies in low orbit just above the cloud tops, the curved horizon of the planet crossing the frame, the viewer feeling the immense scale of the world below.

THE LOOK: one frame from a top-budget science-fiction film, seen through a virtual camera flying through the solar system. Photoreal, physically based: real light, real materials, real depth, as if the viewer is there. Ultra-wide lens with an immense sense of scale. The Sun is the only key light: hard directional sunlight, a strong rim light, long shadows, deep contrast, with detail held in both the brightest and the darkest areas. Rich, saturated color pushed a little past reality without turning garish. Space is alive: a dense field of pin-sharp stars of varied brightness and tint, the dusty glowing band of the Milky Way crossing the background, faint nebular glow and drifting dust. Extreme fine detail at every scale, layered depth, volumetric haze where there is atmosphere. Natural lens bloom on the brightest highlights. It must not look like a spacecraft photograph, a textbook illustration, a painting or a stylized render, and nothing may look flat, plastic or plain. No text, labels, borders, watermarks, interface elements, spacecraft or people. 16:9 landscape.

THE SHOT: looking forward toward the Sun, which sits just above the top edge of the frame, off-frame; the horizon curves gently across the upper third of the frame, tilted a few degrees; the lower two-thirds is the ocean and coast rushing toward and under the camera.

THE SURFACE: deep sapphire ocean with a real, recognizable coastline: the Bahamas banks and the coast of Florida, vivid turquoise shallows against the deep blue of the ocean trench. Turquoise and jade shallows over sand banks and reefs, white surf lines, river mouths spilling silt plumes into the sea, green-gold land with real terrain relief. A blinding sun glint blazes on the water, a molten white-gold path across the sea in which every wave and current line shows as fine silver texture. Rising out of the cloud field below, towering cumulonimbus thunderheads with flat anvil tops and overshooting domes stand like mountain ranges, sculpted in volumetric cauliflower detail, their sunward sides blazing white-gold and their far sides in cool blue shadow; each thunderhead casts an enormous long shadow streaming across the lower clouds and the sea. Fields of small cumulus and thin cirrus streaks add layered depth down to the sea. All cloud is soft vapour: no pits, no dimples, no hard-rimmed spots.

THE HORIZON: the curved limb of the Earth, with the atmosphere glowing electric blue in thin layered shells, from a bright white-cyan line at the horizon through deep cobalt into black space. Above it, black space with delicate pin-sharp stars, much dimmer than the sunlit planet.

LIGHT: one hard, distant Sun ahead of the camera, just off the top of the frame, back-lighting the thunderheads so their tops glow with a burning rim and their shadows stream toward the camera; the sun glint runs up the middle of the frame toward the horizon. Low sun, so every cloud edge is rim-lit and every shadow is long. Everything in sharp focus from foreground to the horizon: no depth-of-field blur, no bokeh, no floating debris.

CAMERA AND GRADE: photographed like a large-format film frame through an anamorphic lens, ultra-wide. A soft warm veil of flare and a horizontal anamorphic streak tied to the sun glint on the water. Cool blue-black shadows against warm gold highlights, very fine film grain, gentle natural vignette. Low-key and high contrast. No spacecraft, no station hardware, no window frame, no solar panels, nothing in the foreground but the planet.
