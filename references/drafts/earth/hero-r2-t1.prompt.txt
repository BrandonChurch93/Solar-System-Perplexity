THE GLOBE: unmistakably Earth, with geographically accurate continents in their true proportions and positions, as on a real globe (the Atlantic Ocean is its true width). On the day side: the eastern Atlantic Ocean, West Africa with the gold Sahara and the green Congo basin, and Europe with the Mediterranean in the upper part. Deep sapphire and cobalt oceans with real depth and a soft specular sheen where the sunlight reflects off the sea; turquoise shallows over continental shelves. Land in natural, rich but believable colours: deep greens of forest, ochre and gold desert, brown mountain ranges with subtle relief; not embossed, not over-sharpened. Brilliant white storm spirals, long frontal cloud bands and fields of cumulus with true three-dimensional height, every cloud casting a fine shadow on the sea below, the cloud relief sharpest along the soft terminator. On the night side: the eastern seaboard of North America, the Caribbean and the coast of Brazil, glittering warm gold and amber city lights strung along coasts, rivers and highways, faint blue-white flickers of lightning inside a few night-side storm clusters, and the land and clouds faintly readable in cool, dim bounce light, not pure black.

THE AURORA: The northern aurora sits on the night side over northern Canada and Hudson Bay, in the upper left part of the globe. It is a thin, low ribbon of light hugging the night-side surface, seen from above as a glowing green oval ring lying flat on the dark hemisphere around the magnetic pole, with fine curtain folds and a faint crimson fringe just above it. Its height is a tiny fraction of the globe's radius: it never spikes out into space, never stands off the limb, and never appears at mid-latitudes.

THE ATMOSPHERE: a thin, luminous electric-blue atmosphere line wraps the whole limb, glowing brightest along the sunlit edge, with layered haze shells, fading to a deep dim blue on the night limb.
