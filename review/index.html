A flight past nine worlds

Solar System Fly-Through

It begins here. On the surface of a star.

    Three minutes. Sound on. The words come as they are spoken.

    Nine worlds

    Each one is seen whole, then from just above its surface, then left behind. Every world was a picture first. Under each frame you can change between the film and the picture it was built from.

    How it was made

    Nothing here was filmed.

    Every frame is drawn by a program, in a web browser, one frame at a time. Three.js draws the worlds, their weather and their light. Then the frames are joined: 5,400 of them, thirty for each second.

    Each world was a picture first.

    Before any code, every world had two approved pictures: one of the whole world, one from just above its surface. The code was written to match them, and then it was flown.

    It is one flight.

    The camera never lands. It comes to a world, holds, dives to the surface, rises and leaves for the next. Nine times, from the Sun to Neptune. Then it keeps going, and looks back.

    The voice.

    Nineteen lines, spoken by a generated voice. Each word comes onto the screen at the moment it is spoken, on a haze of violet. There is no box around the words.

    The music.

    One piece of music, fitted to the length of the flight. The voice is laid over it.

    The same every time.

    The film is not a recording of a screen. The program can draw any frame again, exactly the same, at any size.
