# 02 · The stops

One section per stop. The approved images are the authority. These notes point at what matters in them and say what has to move.

- File paths of the approved images are in `stops` in the config.
- "Keep" lists what the orbit view must share with the approved hero image at the midpoint of the `hold`.
- "Technique" is the starting technique for the terrain view. `kit/03-METHOD.md` explains each one. The ladder in `kit/04-LOOK.md` says when to change.
- Bodies are recognizable at a glance and exaggerated beyond reality. Do not correct an approved image toward science.

## Shared rules

- Every globe turns during its `hold`, slowly, in its real direction.
- Every moon moves along its orbit, slowly.
- The night side is never pure black. It carries faint detail and whatever lights the stop lists.
- The limb of every body with an atmosphere glows, as in its approved hero image.

## Sun

- Keep: the full disc, deeply textured, with dark sunspot clusters, white-hot active regions, prominences leaping off the edge on both sides, and long corona rays reaching into space. The surface detail stays visible. Nothing blows out to flat white.
- Light: the Sun is the light. Bloom and glare come from it.
- Motion in the hold: granulation boils. Bright regions pulse. Plasma streams along the prominences and rains back down. Corona rays shimmer and drift.
- Spot: the sunspot under the plasma loops in the approved close image.
- Technique: relief with flow. Granulation cells churn. The sunspot's filaments creep inward. Plasma runs along each loop from one foot to the other. Loops are their own layers in front of the surface.
- Cover for `sun.rise`: a flare. The picture blooms to bright gold for a moment and resolves into the whole disc.
- Note: frame 0 is in `sun.terrain`. It is the poster frame.

## Mercury

- Keep: the battered grey and bronze globe, lit hard from one side, with the enormous Sun glaring at the edge of frame and a strong flare.
- Light: the harshest of the film. Razor shadows, no atmosphere, no glow at the limb.
- Motion in the hold: the globe turns. The Sun's glare breathes and its flare shifts as the camera drifts.
- Spot: the large terraced crater in the approved close image, placed near the terminator on the globe.
- Technique: relief. Crater rims and terraces carry strong parallax. Nothing on the ground moves. The sky above the horizon is the live sky with the Sun's glow on the horizon.
- Cover: the Sun's glare sweeps across the lens during the fastest part of the dive.

## Venus

- Keep: the golden globe wrapped in cloud, the chevron bands, the spiral vortex near the pole, the thick glowing limb.
- Light: warm. The terminator is soft and wide.
- Motion in the hold: the cloud bands drift, fastest at the equator. Faint lightning flickers inside the clouds on the night side, seeded.
- Spot: the cloud towers and canyons in the approved close image, with the Sun low on the horizon.
- Technique: relief with flow. Cloud towers are layers with depth. Haze drifts between them. The shafts of light sway very slightly.
- Cover: the camera punches through a layer of bright haze.

## Earth

- Keep: the Americas facing the camera, the hurricane with its eye, turquoise shallows, the blue line of atmosphere, the terminator with gold city lights on the night side, green aurora at the pole, the Moon small in the distance.
- Light: clean white sunlight. The most familiar world, so the most carefully judged.
- Motion in the hold: clouds drift over the surface as their own layer and cast shadows. The hurricane turns. City lights twinkle faintly. The aurora ripples. The Moon moves.
- Moon: `moon`. Grey and cratered, far off, lit from the same side as Earth.
- Spot: the tropical coast in the approved close image. Place it in the Caribbean on the globe.
- Technique: relief with flow. Thunderheads are tall layers that cast long shadows on the sea. The sun glint shimmers. Cloud shadows creep. The blue limb glows.
- Cover: the camera passes through a thin veil of high cloud.

## Mars

- Keep: rust, ochre and crimson. Valles Marineris as a long scar across the disc, the Tharsis volcanoes, the white polar cap, a curling dust storm, thin haze at the limb, and Phobos large and dark in the foreground, rim-lit.
- Light: warm and low in contrast on the day side, with long shadows near the terminator.
- Motion in the hold: the globe turns. The dust storm churns slowly. Phobos drifts across the disc and tumbles a little.
- Moon: `phobos`. It is close to the camera and large in frame, so it is a real 3D object with craters, grooves and the Stickney crater. A flat card will not do.
- Spot: Valles Marineris. The dive follows the canyon in.
- Technique: relief with flow. Canyon walls and mesas carry strong parallax. Mist drifts along the canyon floor. Dust hangs in the air and catches the low sun.
- Cover: a band of dust haze at the top of the atmosphere.
- Note: Mars is the look lock stop. It is built first and reviewed by Brandon before any other stop is finished.

## Jupiter

- Keep: cream, amber and rust bands with turbulent edges, the Great Red Spot, white ovals, and Io in front of the disc casting a crisp black shadow on the cloud tops.
- Light: soft terminator, deep night side.
- Motion in the hold: neighboring bands flow in opposite directions. The Great Red Spot turns. Io and its shadow cross the disc.
- Moon: `io`. Yellow and mottled. Its shadow on Jupiter is computed, not painted, so it moves with the moon.
- Spot: the Great Red Spot.
- Technique: relief with flow. The storm rotates round its own center. Cloud walls have depth. Smaller vortices turn. Faint lightning glows inside the darker clouds, seeded.
- Cover: the camera drops through a layer of pale ammonia haze.

## Saturn

- Keep: the pale gold banded globe inside wide open rings, the dark Cassini Division, the rings' shadow across the globe, the globe's shadow across the rings, and Titan small and orange in the distance.
- Light: the two shadows are the picture. Both are computed from the Sun's direction, so they stay right as the camera moves.
- Motion in the hold: the rings turn, inner parts faster than outer. The bands on the globe drift. Titan moves.
- Moon: `titan`. Hazy and orange.
- Spot: the ring plane. The dive goes to the rings, not to the globe.
- Technique: true 3D. The camera skims just above the rings with Saturn huge behind, as in the approved close image. The rings resolve into thousands of fine bands, and near the lens into drifting grains of ice that sparkle. Change to a relief only if true 3D cannot match the approved close image inside the round limit.
- Cover: none is needed while the terrain view is true 3D. The dive is one continuous move.

## Uranus

- Keep: the smooth pale cyan globe with soft banding, bright cloud flecks and a brighter pole, thin rings standing nearly vertical, and small moons nearby.
- Light: cool. The globe is tilted on its side, so the rings stand upright in frame.
- Motion in the hold: the bands drift. The cloud flecks move. The moons move. The thin rings glint as the camera drifts.
- Moons: `miranda` and `ariel`. Small, grey and cratered.
- Spot: the upper haze near the limb, under the ring arcs.
- Technique: relief with flow for the haze and cloud streaks. The ring arcs overhead are true 3D, shared with the orbit view, so they move correctly against the sky.
- Cover: a layer of pale cyan haze.

## Neptune

- Keep: the deep electric blue globe, streaks of brilliant white cirrus, the dark storm, a faint ring, Triton pale in the distance, and the Sun as a small bright star.
- Light: cold. The Sun is a star, yet the day side is well exposed.
- Motion in the hold: the cirrus streams fast. The dark storm turns. Triton moves.
- Moon: `triton`. Pale, with a pinkish cap.
- Spot: the dark storm, with the white cloud band streaming round it.
- Technique: relief with flow. The white clouds are a raised layer that casts shadows on the blue deck below. They stream along the band. The storm turns slowly.
- Cover: a layer of blue haze.
- Note: `neptune.rise` leads into the ending. There is no `travel`.
