## Issues and what changes

1. Major, whole film: every stop has the same grammar, a centered globe, a straight scale-up, terrain, an edge exit, bare stars. Change: each stop now arrives on its own curve. The camera swings round the body while it closes in (`arrive` in each stop's plan). Saturn arrives in the ring plane and the rings open as it climbs to the hero view. Jupiter arrives from the night side. Venus comes up from below, Mars and Neptune from opposite sides. Mercury, Earth lightly and Uranus stay straight. Turn angles differ from stop to stop (66 to 92 degrees) and so do the turn lengths. Segment times are unchanged: Brandon judges the pacing from the rough cut, and a retime would move every later judged frame.
2. Major, `ending.lookback`: Neptune cropped at the left edge, the Sun too small to be a second subject, no reframe. Change: Neptune sits 18.5 degrees off axis with its whole disc clear of the edge, the Sun's glare is raised to 1.5 with a small streak, and the camera eases a 3.5 degree pan toward the Sun over the first 4.5 seconds, then holds.
3. Major, `mars.travel`: Mars left abruptly, then stars only, then Jupiter grew from a point to full size in a few frames. Change: the turn is smaller (86 degrees), the approach starts the moment the departing body has left frame (computed per stop, 0.20 to 0.34 of the travel), and the growth follows `1 - (1 - x)^2.5`, so the body is a tenth of its hero size after about 40 percent of the approach and the last half of the travel is a long ease. The point of light now fades into the disc over a wider range of sizes, so it no longer dims at the handover.
4. Minor, `mars.dive`: no landmark is carried through the cover. Left for Phase 3, where the detail patches show the canyon from above and the dive can hold one bend in place.
5. Minor, `sun.terrain`: little change across the opening. Change: a lateral creep (1.1 units with 3 degrees of yaw) and a longer push. Real parallax between the sunspot and the loops arrives with the Sun's relief in Phase 4.
